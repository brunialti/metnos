# RM-0008 — G8 work plan

Date: 15 September 2026.
Source baseline: `06a9472e`.
Status: **in progress; not F5 certification or activation**.
Assignment and approved principles: [decision record](decision_rm0008_g8_scope_15_9_2026.md).

## 1. Scope

Complete G8 before G9. F6 remains assigned to Codex after F5, but no G10
schema or deletion authority is introduced here. Do not repeat the F4
transition or modify the shared root checkout.

G8 has two outputs: an installation-bound, evidence-derived entry proof and
a lossless, reconciled retirement of name-based lifecycle state. A signed
summary, a green isolated test suite, or copying rows alone is not either
output. The detailed missing contracts remain enumerated in the handover
and decision record; they must be closed before their implementation.

Roberto's clarification on 16 September: avoid unnecessary restrictions that
block normal use. Historical acquisition/verification is a certification
workflow, not a new bootstrap/routing prerequisite or user-permission gate.
A missing historical proof limits the certification claim; it must not stop
ordinary services, disable unrelated executors or invent extra consent steps.
Preserve benign structural states explicitly. Refuse the specific claim or
operation where integrity, exact identity or authority cannot be established;
do not broaden a local finding into an unrelated system-wide denial. Existing
productive safety requirements and the approved F5 entry threshold remain.

## 2. Immediate prerequisite: faithful terminal replay

This bounded repair strengthens an existing F4 reader; it neither opens F5
nor changes a signer, schema, capability or productive caller.

- Failure location: `executor_birth_operational._decode_terminal_envelope`
  constructs the publication using the request's contract without comparing
  the contract encoded in the signed publication. `_replay_terminal` also
  does not compare the committed claim's `result_binding` with the envelope.
- Observed fact: the serializer includes both contract identity and an exact
  envelope hash in the durable result, but these two bindings are not both
  checked on replay. Existing signature-tampering tests do not cover a
  correctly signed but inconsistent contract or a modified stored hash.
- Causal hypothesis: the reconstructed value can mask an inconsistent signed
  identity; independent postcondition verification then sees the reconstructed
  request identity instead of the contradictory signed fact.
- Expected result: reject either mismatch before postcondition verification,
  checks or publication; preserve the durable record. Valid exact replay,
  historical-key rotation, signed rejection and crash recovery must retain
  their existing behavior.

Contract: schema 2 and the existing signing/hash domains stay unchanged.
The publication's encoded `contract_id` must equal the request's exact
`ContractId.value` before construction. For a committed claim, the stored
`result_binding` must equal `_terminal_binding(encoded)`. A rejected claim
must not be treated as a successful committed publication. No inference from
names, replacement signatures, fallback values or new key is permitted.

Tests: one parametrized negative replay family for the contract, result hash
and terminal-state bindings, followed by the operational/Producer replay and recovery
families whose boundary is affected. Record the red baseline before repair.
This is an isolated productive-code traversal, not a live installation or
an admission qualifying for the F5 threshold. Installed and native Windows
verification remain unproven until actually performed.

Independent internal review found one remaining state ambiguity on checkpoint
`04aa1b14`: an authentic intermediate admitted hint, stored as `rejected`,
has no publication and therefore passed the publication-presence comparison.
The result still reported `admitted` with no operational error. The affected
symbol is `_replay_terminal`; the cause is that publication presence alone
does not distinguish an intermediate hint from a final rejection.

The additional repair must compare the signed outcome and operational error
with the terminal state: committed means admitted/preexercise with no error;
rejected means rejected/needs_human/quarantined with an operational error equal
to the stored rejection code. This does not require equality between the
original check error and a later finalization error. Keep legitimate recovery
hints usable only by the existing in-progress recovery path. Extend the same
negative replay family, record the new red cases, and exercise the positive
rejection/recovery paths affected by this extra comparison.

## 3. G8 contract candidate for independent review

This section makes the approved principles executable at their owner
boundaries. It is a contract candidate, not an installed authority or a
claim that the complete G8 design has passed independent review. The
remaining freeze items in section 6 must be resolved before their code.

### 3.1 Historical verification and admission predicate — N2/N3

Use separate historical readers, not a historical object passed off as a
current selection. The ownership-chain reader authenticates the F4 anchor
and every relevant successor context; `executor_birth_prepared_root` returns
only the public verifier material pinned by those authenticated contexts.
Never return `SealedAuthoritiesV1`, call the private-key loader and discard
the key afterwards, or require an obsolete signing private key to verify
a historical signature. Historical author rotation must use the historical
public inventory, not equality with today's active author key.

`contract_store` owns complete enumeration of admission receipts and their
generation/source/journal bindings. This means persistent signed bindings,
not a claim that the original temporary source or journal still exists.
Roberto explicitly approved this distinction after the internal review;
see the decision record's historical-evidence clarification.
`executor_birth_producer_store` owns the
matching Producer bytes, issuance and terminal rows. Its historical reader
opens the existing database read-only, requires the supported schema, starts
one read transaction and never calls `_open`, `_migrate` or `_verify_claimed`.
The enclosing certifier must prove a common stable frontier; two independent
SQLite snapshots are not a cross-store snapshot.

The proposed qualifying predicate is the conjunction of:

- a valid historical `AdmissionReceipt` with `kind=admission`;
- revision class in `first_birth`, `code_revision`, `authority_revision`,
  `contract_revision`, and `approved_lifecycle=active`;
- a verified Producer receipt and its capability-specific issuance, with
  exact request, contract, objective, source, issuer and receipt-byte binding;
- a committed, authenticated schema-2 terminal with no error, report outcome
  `admitted`, and publication operation `commit_birth_snapshot`;
- equality of all overlapping signed facts: request, contract, generation,
  predecessor, candidate, semantic core, context, revision class, checks and
  embedded admission bytes, plus the exact terminal hash;
- Producer validity at the signed admission act time, not the database's
  unsigned finalization time or an invented time immediately before expiry;
- independently reread persistent receipts/generation payloads and agreement
  of their authenticated source/journal bindings, with no pending ambiguous
  recovery. Original-byte availability is recorded separately: a signed hash
  is not presented as a material reread of an absent original.

Localizations, equivalent publication, promotion, reactivation,
reattestation, rejected/quarantined results and preexercise do not qualify
under this proposed predicate. They remain in the enumerated evidence with
an exclusion reason. A malformed or unauthenticated item is not silently
discarded to make the remaining inventory appear complete.

Important source distinction: ordinary technical admissions still use the
request-v1 derivation in `executor_birth_bootstrap._request_factory`, even
after an F4 context transition. `ProducerRequestV2` is used by the context
reattestation path. Do not require a forged V2 request for ordinary technical
history. Resolve capability from the context's closed producer table and
authenticated key binding; a mutable `capability_id` string alone is not proof.

The owner must distinguish the protocols actually persisted. The V2
reattestation path stores an admission receipt and its
`reattestation-result/v1` binding without a schema-2 terminal envelope; verify
that protocol before recording it as excluded. Conversely, an ordinary
committed terminal may omit the embedded admission when the publisher reused
an existing receipt. Reread that exact durable receipt independently; never
invent missing embedded bytes or treat either valid protocol as corruption.

For ordinary request-v1, recompute the request digest using the factory's
domain plus unsigned 64-bit big-endian UTF-8 field lengths and field bytes:
issuer, capability operation, exact contract, objective and candidate-source
ID. This is `executor_birth_bootstrap._hash`, not the typed
`encode_framed_v1` codec used for Producer store names. Verify the Producer
nonce against the factory's SHA-256 request-ID
derivation as well. The signed admission `issued_at` is the core's act time
captured before checks, not an independently attested finalization timestamp.

Candidate/source identities include the original code bytes. When those
originals are absent, verify authenticated cross-bindings under the approved
historical-evidence option and state that limitation explicitly; do not claim
to recompute either identity from a manifest's code digest alone.

Count each admitted act once across receipt ID, request ID and exact
`(ContractId,generation_id)`. Conflicting duplicates fail; exact duplicates,
retries and context reattestations do not add admissions. Count producers
by authenticated `issuer_id`, not operation, key, directory or executor name.

### 3.2 Certification authority and activation — N1/N2

Proposed scope: one dedicated administrative F5 certification identity,
separate from author, Admission, Producer, distribution, cutover, head and
HTTP credentials. It can attest the threshold evidence only; it cannot
publish executors, change lifecycle, grant capabilities or delete objects.
Provisioning belongs to the native administrative installer. No candidate,
model or ordinary runtime request can create, select or replace this identity.

Private custody belongs outside the service-owned roots; Linux requires the
existing administrator-owned storage discipline and mode 0600, Windows an
equivalent native ACL proof. Runtime readers receive public material only.
Reuse existing canonical Ed25519 codecs and protected filesystem primitives;
do not add a general certificate service or a second installation manager.

The bounded dossier must bind policy/schema version, installation identity,
F4 anchor, authenticated chain frontier, observed source/build, complete
owner inventories, admitted/excluded act identities, both cycle IDs/profile,
defect-census/ledger head, public-registry revision and certifier identity.
Lists and counts are derived from verified owner records, never supplied as
authority by the caller. Canonical encoding rejects duplicate/unknown fields,
invalid types, truncation and inconsistent references. Large history is
paged by the owner and completely reconciled before a positive result.

Keep the historical threshold proof separate from current-release activation.
The runtime's fixed-root reader verifies the selected installation/head,
policy compatibility and current revocation state before returning a sealed
activation object. A later release does not inherit activation merely because
it descends from the certified one. Neither object hashes a manifest that
already includes that object's own digest. No permissive v1 compatibility
wrapper is needed: `load_f5_activation` has no productive caller today.

### 3.3 Complete cycles and defect evidence — N4/N5

Use the existing external HTTP certification harness where it covers the
required boundary. Register the exact versioned case/profile digest and
expected postconditions before starting; do not call two selected successful
turns a complete cycle. Fixture paths, actors and effects must remain isolated
and non-personal; no external messages or new paid API is authorized.

Record every cycle start and terminal outcome, including failure and
interruption, in sequence. The qualifying pair consists of two adjacent
complete successes on the same authenticated source/catalog/profile. A
profile or base change starts a different sequence; an unexplained difference
blocks qualification. Record real turn references and independently observed
postconditions, not just a harness return code.

Initialize the defect ledger from the known RM-0008 findings, including the
terminal-replay repair in section 2. Use an append-only, authenticated record
of opening, closing and reopening. A closure needs causal evidence, affected
tests, installed verification where required and independent review. A
developer's empty list or a scalar zero cannot establish completeness.
Reopening a relevant defect invalidates a positive current-frontier result.

### 3.4 Lossless migration and retirement — N6/N7/N8

The new epoch store belongs to the service and resolves its path through
central configuration. Version 2 cannot silently gain new meanings: freeze
version 3 explicitly before implementing historical associations and the
cutover record. Keep original copied rows immutable; store any later exact
association and its evidence separately. Retrying preservation must verify
the original bytes even after a legitimate resolution was appended.

Copy all `executor_stats`, `executor_history` and `proposal_promote` rows,
including duplicates/history, with source identity, schema fingerprint,
stable row order, count and digest, followed by independent destination
reread. Preserve source databases. `unresolved` is valid for history without
proof; it is not permission to drop effective restrictions or pending work.
New current epochs come from authenticated current generations, with zero
inherited counters and no inferred historical `name -> current` mapping.

Prefer a short, explicitly managed maintenance window for the one-time
migration over a continuously running synchronization subsystem. The writer
census must cover direct SQL as well as aging/promoter APIs, all services,
scheduled callbacks and administrative commands. The barrier must remain
effective across interruption and cover already-open database connections.
Individual SQLite locks or an in-memory flag do not establish that barrier.

Before disabling a legacy reader, determine which stored restrictions are
still effective against the authenticated current catalog. Preserve each
effective restriction with an explicit exact-generation disposition; if the
binding cannot be established, stop that cutover. A new present-time
disposition must not be misrepresented as proof of a historical association.

Drain or explicitly dispose of open promotion/grace/review work before
retirement. Never turn an already active generation into preexercise.
No live row is automatically discarded because its old mechanism is retiring.
Only after complete reconciliation, necessary dispositions and durable
cutover may legacy writers become unreachable/refuse mutations and legacy
views become non-decisional. Crash/retry must not reopen those writers.

### 3.5 Closed prerequisite slice: public historical context reader

The independent reviewer completed the concrete reader contract after
Roberto's historical-evidence approval. This slice introduces no signer,
certificate, activation, mutation or candidate-selected root. It does not
freeze the remaining certification/ledger/migration contracts.

- `executor_birth_context_v1` decodes the existing canonical material schema:
  reconstruct historical context/epoch from the recorded components, and
  recompute the registry component whose complete configuration is retained.
  Other component source hashes remain historical bindings, not reread code.
  Do not compare historical component versions or source lists with today's
  catalog. Require exact field sets, types, bounded bytes and canonical JSON.
- `executor_birth_prepared_set` reads a historical public set through an
  already locked session, checking set/content identity, mandatory chain pins,
  registry documents, sorted unique key IDs and self-identifying public bytes.
  Preserve the authenticated `producer_id:operation -> store_name` mapping;
  do not construct operational capabilities or infer a current producer table.
- `executor_birth_prepared_root.load_historical_context_verifiers_v1(context_id)`
  treats its argument only as a selector. Internally read the fixed ownership
  chain before and after, select only an unambiguous authenticated transition,
  and return an immutable, inert public view with frontier/transition/set/
  context/epoch and verified document hashes. No caller-supplied chain, root,
  callback, `PreparedSetV1`, `ContextSelectionV1` or `SealedAuthoritiesV1`.
- Remove the private dependency also from the existing
  `_historical_birth_public_inventory_v1`, which the chain's public registry
  reader calls. Use the fixed marker and the same public set reader, without
  calling the chain recursively. Its key-exclusion result includes all current
  self-identifying author public files as well as the marker-set keys. Extra
  author keys can only increase the forbidden set; they never enter a
  historical verification ring not declaring them.

Keep private runtime loading unchanged. The historical reader never reads
`keystore.json` or `private/*`, nor compares with today's active author key.
Existing global locking, protected handle-relative reads, domains, set schema
and context schema are reused. New result types are not signing authorities.

One new discriminating family covers valid history with private access denied
and author rotation; rejection of altered set/material/key/Producer binding;
unknown or ambiguous context; changed chain frontier; immutable result and
no writes. Reuse one existing key-exclusion traversal after changing that
dependency, not all F4 certification tests. Structural boundary review is
required because the read graph changes. Installed and Windows proof remain
open until run.

Explicit limits: this first reader selects target contexts present in the
authenticated F4 chain; the predecessor of its first transition is not
automatically covered. The existing Ownership key-exclusion check does not
cover Admission/Producer keys of later sets; retain that as an N1 finding,
not a guarantee supplied by this slice. Neither limit permits incomplete
history to be reported as complete F5 evidence.

### 3.7 Closed prerequisite slice: public terminal evidence — N3

The independent reviewer accepted a single shared schema-2 decoder in
`executor_birth_operational`. Its inputs are bytes, an expected request ID
and exact `ContractId`, not a fabricated `BirthRequest` or `ManifestRef`.
`verify_terminal_evidence_v1` verifies with the public verifier mapping
provided by the owning caller; it neither establishes that mapping's
authority nor qualifies an Admission. The operational wrapper supplies its
existing core-owned public ring and uses this same decoder.

Freeze the actual emitted field sets and types, with canonical ASCII JSON,
a 4 MiB envelope limit before parsing and on emission, and a 64-byte Ed25519
signature. Reuse the existing signature/hash domains and canonical codec.
Validate nullable digests, unique string lists/check identities, existing
enum values, canonical base64 and optional diagnostic objects. Do not use
today's check catalog or constrain operation names to the observed history.
Preserve valid hints, rejection families, preexercise and publication with
no embedded receipt. Embedded Admission bytes still need their own verifier.

Only publication carries a signed `contract_id` in this wire schema. A
report's reconstructed contract is caller context, not an independently
signed fact when publication is absent. Likewise `repeated=True`, report
schema and publisher-call count are reconstruction defaults, not proof of
retries or successful publication. State/hash/postcondition checks remain
at operational replay; exact issuance/Admission bindings remain at N3.

One new discriminating family covers pure public verification and malformed
signed documents. Reuse the affected operational replay/recovery tests after
the shared decoder changes; do not rerun unrelated public-context tests.

### 3.8 Producer history acquisition — N3

The store owner provides `read_producer_history_v1` at the selected
`config.PATH_USER_STATE` root, with no caller-supplied database, SQL, callback
or signing authority. Move the unchanged Birth/Producer basename constants
from bootstrap into this owner and reuse them in bootstrap. This is raw
evidence acquisition, not an authenticated admission or a sealed F5 result.
The call requires an already initialized, selected runtime configuration.
Product module imports currently reach `config.ensure_dirs`; a cold
administrative certifier must install its no-mutation protection **before**
those imports. The API alone does not promise cold-import purity.

Return separate immutable receipt and issuance records with every stored
column and their original row IDs. Include all states and unmatched records;
no inner join, filtering, recovery, expiry substitution or implicit cleanup.
Require schema 5, real tables and exact supported column shapes. Preserve
the table definitions. An unsupported schema, malformed stored value,
missing database or exceeded budget is an explicit failure, not an empty or
partially successful inventory.

Use one `mode=ro`/`query_only` transaction for both tables, deterministic row
order and metadata-only byte/type checks before loading row payloads. Default
budgets are 100,000 total rows, 128 MiB total field bytes and 15 seconds, with
an 8 MiB per-row field-byte limit (SQLite receives an additional 4 KiB for
record headers). Table SQL is bounded to 64 KiB before materialization.
Positive caller budgets may narrow or widen
the inventory bound, never bypass the fixed per-row limit. Check selected
root/database identity and restrictive metadata before and after; do not
create or repair paths. Refuse linked/nonregular database or sidecar files.

This is a logical SQLite snapshot, not a physical no-write guarantee:
SQLite can maintain its WAL shared-memory sidecars during a read. Keep normal
locking; do not use `immutable` or `nolock` for the live database. See the
[SQLite WAL documentation](https://sqlite.org/wal.html#read_only_databases)
and [URI immutability contract](https://sqlite.org/uri.html#uriimmutable).
The reader does not prove a common contract/Producer/chain frontier, prevent
every transient namespace substitution or establish native Windows ACL
protection. Those remain explicit certification requirements.

One new isolated family covers complete/raw inventory, WAL-consistent reads
across concurrent commits, absent/unsupported/malformed sources, links and
replacement, and exact/over-budget boundaries. Exercise only the bootstrap
tests affected by relocating the constants. No installed qualification is
claimed from these fixtures.

The independent reviewer accepted this bounded contract, including the
explicit initialized-runtime/cold-import distinction. No additional
authority, normative waiver or installed certification follows from it.

### 3.9 Exact historical publication read-back — N3

`contract_store.read_historical_birth_evidence_v1` accepts an exact
`ContractId`, generation digest and an explicit context digest or `None`.
It resolves only the configured store: `None` selects the original V1
receipt layout; a context selects V2, without fallback to another context.
There is no caller root, invented `ManifestRef`, current-generation check,
private authority, publication lock creation or operational recovery.

Return immutable raw receipt bytes and the three exact stored generation
payloads. Reuse the owner's structural binding, path and generation digest
codecs. Bounded regular-file reads reject links, hardlinks and changed files;
binding is limited to 64 KiB, receipt/manifest/language state to 1 MiB each,
and the Ed25519 signature to exactly 64 bytes. Require the exact generation
file set and matching generation digest. Observe directory identities around
acquisition and independently reread the binding and receipt.

This proves exact bounded acquisition and the generation's content address,
not authenticity of receipt/author signatures or historical policy. It does
not parse old declarations using today's executor standard or claim absent
code/journal originals were preserved. Signature/context/act verification,
complete enumeration (including retired/unreachable contracts), common
frontier and native ACL certification remain the enclosing N3 work. The
already-initialized-runtime precondition from §3.8 applies here too.

One isolated family covers both layouts, non-current generations, no V2
fallback, all size and identity boundaries, missing/corrupt durable bytes,
link/substitution attempts and refusal of mutating/current readers. Existing
current-generation validation and publication remain unchanged.

Independent review accepted this bounded acquisition contract and found no
blocker in the implementation. The complete inventory must observe both
stored layouts explicitly: a terminal carrying a context does not establish
that its durable receipt was stored under the V2 path. Request protocol,
receipt layout and cryptographic context are separate facts.

### 3.10 Complete raw receipt inventory — N3

Extend the same store owner with `read_historical_birth_inventory_v1`.
Resolve the configured store only; enumerate every physical contract,
including contracts absent from the live catalog or with retired pointers.
Derive identity from the existing bounded canonical binding, never an
executor name, current `ManifestRef` or terminal observation. Enumerate both
stored receipt layouts independently, preserving every exact locator and
raw receipt, including duplicate, rejected or otherwise ineligible acts.
Empty receipt sets are explicit, not omitted contracts.

The immutable inventory contains its selected source path, per-contract raw
binding, separate generation/retirement ID censuses, and raw V1/V2 receipt
records. Distinguish the exact existing three-file generation and two-file
retirement shapes; observe their regular-file metadata without reading or
authenticating payloads. Receipt generation locators must name a generation,
not a tombstone. The exact-item reader owns admitted-generation bytes.
Coexisting V1/V2 locators remain separate even when receipt bytes are equal;
deduplication belongs to the enclosing certifier.
Do not reopen those payloads once per historical context merely to enumerate
receipts. Signature, act, policy and complete-certification validation remain
separate consumers, not claims of this acquisition API.

Fail without a partial return on an absent root, unknown/staging entries in
the traversed namespaces, malformed bindings/locators, links, unsafe file
types or permissions, missing referenced generations, or exhausted budgets.
Known `current`/`writer.lock` metadata may be observed without using either
as a selection, acquiring a lock, creating a file or recovering publication.
Only `binding.json` and `generations` are required at contract level; a
contract with no current pointer is still history, not silently excluded.
An existing and actually empty root returns an explicit empty inventory.

Default bounds are 100,000 traversed entries, 128 MiB of acquired binding/
receipt/observed-lock bytes and a 15-second cooperative deadline. File bounds reuse §3.9.
Bound scans before sorting/materializing; refuse rather than truncate.
Observe every traversed directory and file before its read/scan and compare
identity, size, change timestamps and directory child sets after acquisition.
This detects ordinary
concurrent namespace/data changes but is not a cross-store transaction,
crash-durable all-writer barrier or protection against every transient ABA
substitution. Cold-import and native Windows limitations remain explicit.

One new inventory family covers all contracts/layouts and empty histories,
deterministic complete output, budgets, malformed/unknown/staging namespaces,
raw failed acts, links and data/membership/replacement races. Reuse the exact
reader's unchanged file-guard evidence; do not repeat its 39 cases merely to
inflate coverage. An installed inventory probe, if run, must report aggregate
acquisition facts, not qualifying-admission counts.

Independent review approved this bounded contract and found no existing
equivalent: the live manifest inventory constructs references from the live
origin map and may enter a locking residual-binding helper;
`diagnose_store` is reference/key-based and treats an absent root as empty.
Neither can supply this complete raw receipt namespace. The review required
the tombstone census and physical-layout distinction made explicit above.

**Reviewed refinement after the installed observation:** the writer creates
the contract/generations directories and lock before binding creation. The
initial inventory contract refused any missing binding; a live diagnostic
found a namespace with just an empty `generations` and a one-byte lock.
This shape is compatible with a pre-binding phase, but proves neither crash
provenance, writer inactivity nor absence of previously deleted history.

Acquire only the exact empty-unbound shape as a **separate opaque record**:
physical storage key, single NUL lock byte, no binding/current/receipts or
other children, empty generations, regular single-link lock and protected
directories. On POSIX require the existing private 0700/0600 modes and the
same owner/group as the selected root. Keep it within the same entry/byte/
time budgets and final directory/file reread. Do not fabricate a `ContractId`,
deduplicate it away, invoke the live catalog's locking helper or clean it up.
Any deviation still refuses the complete acquisition. The output explicitly
lists `unbound_empty_namespaces`, not verified crash residues. Native Windows
ACL validation remains outside this raw observation.

This is an acquisition refinement, not a certification waiver: the certifier
must reconcile the opaque observations and Producer history under its common
barrier before treating the installation as certification-ready. No new
authority or product-specific locator exception is introduced. Independent
review recommended this explicit preservation over deleting data merely to
permit acquisition; installed results before this refinement remain failures.

### 3.11 Initial-context public keys — N3

The §3.5 selector covers authenticated transition targets only. Complete
history can also refer to the predecessor of the first transition. Extend
the public evidence selector to that one additional authenticated edge,
without selecting an executable old runtime or rerunning the F4 transition.

The existing private selector gains an explicit opt-in for the first
predecessor, disabled by default. Only the public-key evidence reader enables
it. Require exactly one match across targets and that predecessor; ambiguous
or unknown contexts fail. The existing historical Producer-author loader
remains target-only: the first successor's distribution does not authenticate
the predecessor's Producer policy merely because it references that context.

For the predecessor, read the existing fixed marker/public-set interface
under the non-creating shared Birth barrier. Match the returned set ID,
context ID and epoch to the first authenticated transition's `previous_*`
facts. This reuses the set's content-address, marker, material and public-key
checks; no current author equality or private keystore lookup is introduced.
Reread the fixed chain around acquisition as before.

Immutable evidence explicitly records `binding_kind` as `transition_target`
or `initial_predecessor`. For the latter, `transition_id` identifies the first
authenticated edge binding the predecessor, not a distribution that issued
its policy. These are public verifier facts only: historical Producer origin/
author scope, cross-store frontier and qualifying acts remain separate work.

One bounded family covers exact predecessor pins, absent/altered markers,
ambiguity, forbidden private/operational readers, changing frontier and the
continued refusal to project a successor's Producer policy backward. Reuse
the unchanged low-level public-set tests; run only affected selector cases.
Any installed proof must select this predecessor explicitly, not repeat the
two transition targets already checked in §5.2.
